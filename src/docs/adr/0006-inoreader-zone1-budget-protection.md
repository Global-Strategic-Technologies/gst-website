# ADR-0006: Inoreader Zone-1 budget protection & circuit breaker

- **Status**: Accepted (2026-05-13, BL-032.5 soak) — extended by BL-032.7 substrate safety + BL-032.75 spend accounting
- **Source initiative**: BL-032.5 testing findings (archived at [`../development/_archive/BL-032_5_TESTING_FINDINGS.md`](../development/_archive/BL-032_5_TESTING_FINDINGS.md)); protections hardened under BL-032.7

## Context

Inoreader's free-tier API budget is zoned, enforced daily per registered app, and shared across **all** GST consumers presenting the same app credentials: Zone-1 covers the expensive read endpoints (`tag/list`, `stream/contents/*`) at 100 calls/day. The BL-032.5 soak window (opened 2026-05-13) and the BL-032.6 demo cycle riding the same substrate established this empirically rather than from documentation:

- **The real cap was confirmed only via out-of-band dashboard inspection.** During the 2026-05-15 demo-day 429 episode, the handler recorded only `status`/`statusText` and silently discarded the `X-Reader-Zone1-Limit` / `X-Reader-Zone1-Usage` / `X-Reader-Limits-Reset-After` headers Inoreader returns on every authenticated response — including 429s. RCA required a manual Inoreader Developer Dashboard lookup ("Zone 1: 100% of 100 requests/day") and stretched from ~30 seconds to >2 hours (findings § T.Z.3).
- **The exhaustion root cause was structural over-fetch**: hourly crons on staging + production, sharing one Inoreader app, burned 12 Zone-1 calls/hour against the shared 100/day cap — ~44% over budget, yielding partial coverage early each UTC day then full-day outage after the cap hit.
- **The counter-as-budget-proxy broke under 429s**: cron `partial` outcomes incremented the day-counter by 6 even when both tiers 429'd and zero content was retrieved, driving the counter to its soft cap on phantom spend (findings § T.Z.1).
- **The breaker was gated on a consumer pattern, not the upstream signal**: cron 429s never called `openCircuit()` — only the live-tool path did — so the cron could 429-loop for hours invisibly, and the first live tool call after recovery then opened the breaker, blanking radar for 6 additional hours on top of the incident (findings § T.Z.2).

## Decision

Budget protection is layered, with every layer keyed to Inoreader's actual quota semantics as observed in the soak:

1. **One exported cap constant.** `ZONE1_DAILY_HARD_CAP = 100` in `mcp-server/src/lib/inoreader-egress.ts` is the single source of truth (BL-032.75 Phase 3). The cron's day-counter soft cap (`mcp:inoreader:day-counter:<date>`, `cron/radar-refresh.ts`) is a self-imposed cron budget whose worst-case pre-flight gate (counter + 6 exceeds cap → skip until UTC-midnight key expiry) is a guard rail, not the enforcement point. Post-T.Z.1, the counter increments only for tiers that returned `ok: true`; 429-rejected tiers consume zero.
2. **Circuit breaker on rate-limit signals** (`mcp-server/src/ratelimit/circuit-breaker.ts`): a 429 sets `mcp:radar:circuit-open` in Upstash with a 6-hour TTL. While open, radar tools return a 503-shaped envelope with `Retry-After`, and radar reads serve cached snapshot data instead of touching Inoreader; the flag self-heals via TTL expiry and fails open when Upstash is unreachable. Since BL-032.7 (T.Z.2), **every** Inoreader call site — cron and live tools alike — routes failures through `handleInoreaderFailure()` in `mcp-server/src/lib/inoreader-failure-handler.ts`, so the breaker opens on the first upstream 429 regardless of which surface saw it.
3. **429 diagnostics are captured, not discarded** (T.Z.3): `inoreader-client.ts` parses the `X-Reader-Zone*` headers into a `RateLimitInfo` envelope plus a body excerpt; the failure handler attaches them as Sentry tags (`inoreader.zone1.usage`, `.zone1.limit`, `.reset_after_seconds`, `inoreader.source`) so the next 429 event is self-explaining.
4. **Complete spend accounting** (BL-032.75): `recordInoreaderEgress()` in `inoreader-egress.ts` is the chokepoint every Worker-side Inoreader call passes through — `cron-radar`, `live-radar`, `http-radar-snapshot`, `401-retry` (Zone-1) and `oauth-refresh` (per-category only; `/oauth2/token` is outside the Zone tables). 429s increment the counters because `X-Reader-Zone1-Usage` shows they consume quota. The local counter is drift-checked against the latest observed header; disagreement beyond ±6 (one full cron firing — recalibrated in BL-032.77 to absorb the parallel-fetch header race) fires a daily-debounced `inoreader.spend.drift` Sentry warning. See `ARCHITECTURE.md` § Inoreader spend accounting and § Rate limiting & Inoreader budget.
5. **Cadence rule: cron interval ≥ cache TTL.** The radar refresh runs `0 */6 * * *`, exactly matching the 6h snapshot TTL — firing faster re-fetches Inoreader against a warm cache, which is what the original hourly cadence did (144 → 24 calls/day per env when corrected). A single-flight Upstash lock keyed on `cron:scheduledTime` (BL-032.77) stops Cloudflare's documented double-invocation of `scheduled` from doubling that spend.
6. **Staging runs no crons.** Both environments share one Inoreader OAuth app, so a staging cron doubles the burn against the same 100/day cap (observed 2026-05-18: day-counter at 36 by 12:30 UTC vs 18 expected). Re-enabling a staging cron requires a separately registered OAuth app first.

Provenance: the day-counter and breaker scaffolding shipped with BL-032/BL-032.5; the T.Z.1/T.Z.2/T.Z.3 hardening (per-tier accounting, unified failure handler, header capture) landed as BL-032.7 on 2026-05-16; the egress chokepoint, exported hard cap, and drift detection landed as BL-032.75; the dedup lock and drift-threshold recalibration as BL-032.77. **Rejected**: a per-consumer Inoreader app split — duplicating the OAuth refresh path cost as much as retiring the website's direct caller entirely, so BL-032.8 instead made the Worker the sole Inoreader identity, putting every consumer behind these protections automatically.

## Consequences

- **Code that carries this decision**: `mcp-server/wrangler.toml` (cadence-rule + staging-no-cron comments), `mcp-server/src/lib/inoreader-client.ts` (`RateLimitInfo` docstring cites § T.Z.3), `mcp-server/src/lib/inoreader-failure-handler.ts` (module docstring cites § T.Z.2), `mcp-server/src/tools/radar-live.ts` (`failureResponse` comment cites § T.Z.2), `mcp-server/src/cron/radar-refresh.ts` (per-tier accounting), and `mcp-server/scripts/Test-Bl0325.ps1` (the batch runner whose findings fed the source log).
- **Alerting binds to the cap constant**: the `inoreader-budget-exhausted` rule (`src/observability/alert-rules.ts`, evaluated by the 15-minute SLO cron) tickets at 70% (70 calls) and pages at 90% (90 calls) of `ZONE1_DAILY_HARD_CAP`.
- **Budget math**: the 6h cron spends 4 firings × 6 calls = 24/day, leaving ~76 Zone-1 calls for live radar tools, the `/radar/snapshot` endpoint, and 401 retries; the per-key radar rate tier (5/min + 50/day) is defense-in-depth so one bearer cannot exhaust the remainder through cold-cache calls.
- **Accepted trade-offs**: a breaker-open window serves up to 6h-stale radar (availability traded for budget); breaker and counters fail open on Upstash outage — observability and guard rails never fail a user request, so a degraded Upstash temporarily reverts to unprotected calls.
- **Revisit triggers**: Inoreader plan/zone-limit changes (`ZONE1_DAILY_HARD_CAP` and the alert thresholds must move together); BL-033 OAuth multiplying authenticated consumers against the same budget; any wish to re-enable the staging cron (requires a second registered Inoreader app first).

### Amendment — 2026-07-27 (BL-091): breaker-open behavior clarified and made uniform

Decision item 2 above reads: _"While open, radar tools return a 503-shaped envelope with `Retry-After`, and radar reads serve cached snapshot data instead of touching Inoreader."_ **Both clauses were stated; only the first was implemented.** `tools/radar-live.ts` checked the breaker as its first statement and returned 503 _before ever reading the cache_, so a warm 6h snapshot went unserved for up to 6 hours. Meanwhile the other two read surfaces — the `gst://radar/*` Resources reader and the `/radar/snapshot` SSR endpoint — had **no breaker check at all** and would fetch Inoreader live on a cold cache during an open window, spending the budget this ADR exists to protect.

BL-091 implements the second clause and **narrows the first**:

- **Uniform rule on every read surface**: breaker open ⇒ read cache only, never upstream. Enforced structurally by a second reader family (`readWireCached`/`readFyiCached` in `content/radar-live-store.ts`) whose miss case is a `cache-empty` failure deliberately **not** assignable to `InoreaderFailure`, so it can never reach `openCircuit`.
- **503 narrows to the cache-empty case.** Its envelope shape is unchanged; it is now the last resort rather than the first check. Successful degraded reads carry `liveInfo.degraded: true` (+ `retryAfterSeconds`); a tier with nothing cached reports `null` for its `fetchedAt`/`cacheHit`.
- **`/radar/snapshot` now also OPENS the breaker** (`InoreaderFailureSource` gains `http-radar-snapshot`). It is the highest-volume Inoreader consumer and was the one surface that could absorb a 429 without tripping the breaker — restoring the § T.Z.2 "every call site routes through `handleInoreaderFailure`" invariant in full.
- **The `:33` trade-off becomes literally true.** "A breaker-open window serves up to 6h-stale radar" was aspirational; it is now the implemented behavior.
- **Regression guard**: `mcp-server/tests/integration/radar-store-callers-breaker-gated.test.ts` fails CI if a module imports the fetch-capable readers without importing `isCircuitOpen` — the omission that produced this defect twice.
- **Operator visibility**: `circuitOpen` on `/health` and a `/status` row, deliberately **not** folded into `health.ok` (already false in this state via `inoreader: 'degraded'`); it is the missing _discriminator_, not a new breach source.

**Rejected — automatic half-open probe.** A trial fetch to detect early Inoreader recovery was designed and cut: it can _extend_ an outage. The probe can succeed on the last unit of Zone-1 headroom, the follow-on refill (wire = `CALLS_PER_WIRE` calls) then 429s, and `openCircuit` **resets the full 6h TTL** rather than preserving the original expiry — so a 30-minute probe loop can hold the breaker open longer than doing nothing. A safe version needs spend-headroom gating, a TTL-preserving re-arm, compare-and-delete close, and `forceRefresh` evidence; filed as a standalone follow-up (BACKLOG BL-091 § follow-up). **Accepted consequence**: nothing repopulates the radar cache while the breaker is open (the cron already skipped), so early recovery is not detected automatically — manual reset (`RATE_LIMITS.md` § Circuit-breaker manual reset) is the documented lever.
